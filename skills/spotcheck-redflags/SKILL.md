# Red flags

The list behind the score. Use this when someone wants to understand what to look
for themselves.

## When to use

- "What should I be looking at?"
- A score came back unclear and the user wants the detail.
- Training someone to spot a fake list without a tool.

## The checklist

### Naming and presentation

- Generic titles: "Viral Hits", "Best of 2026", "Trending Now".
- Emoji walls or keyword stuffing in the title.
- No description, or a description that promises streams.
- A cover image that is stock art or a logo with no identity.

### Growth shape

- Followers climbing in a straight vertical line over days.
- A jump at exactly the same time every week, which suggests a purchase cycle.
- Long flat periods then sudden spikes.

### Engagement

- Follower count far above what the track saves and streams support.
- Almost no comments, shares, or saves.
- The same handful of accounts interacting every time.

### Curator footprint

- Anonymous account with no name, no history, and one list.
- No other playlists, or many near-identical ones.
- Cannot be found anywhere outside Spotify.

### Behaviour

- Tracks added and removed on a daily cycle.
- Unrelated genres stacked together with no logic.
- The same track appearing in many lists run by the same account.

### The pitch

- Promises a specific number of streams.
- Asks for payment by transfer with no invoice.
- Will not share the playlist before payment.
- Says "guaranteed" about anything.

## Hard rules

- A red flag is not proof. Two flags mean look closer. Four mean walk away.
- Never call a curator a fraud without evidence. Describe the signal, not the person.

## Output

The flags present, grouped by category, then a one-line read: clean, unclear, or
walk away.
